- **Seat:** Devil's Advocate (DA), fixed seat, no configuration card

- **Date:** 21 November 2026
- **Manuscript:** "Do Fixed-Noise and Interacting-Multiple-Model Tracker Covariances Improve Planning Decisions? An Equal-Caution Test on Fresh Simulation Runs, in Closed Loop and on Real Driving Logs" (MANUSCRIPT_v13, SUPPLEMENT_v13, Figs. 1-4), treated as a fresh submission to IEEE OJ-ITS
- **Calibration status:** `NOT_CALIBRATED`
- **Sources read:** the manuscript, the supplement, the four figures and the role definition. Nothing else. No external service was used. Arithmetic checks are stated where used.

What the paper does well, briefly: it reports failed hypotheses instead of hiding them, prints the cost of its own best source behind non-braking leads, lists every departure from its plan, and says plainly that nothing here justifies removing a covariance margin. The criticism below is about what the confirmed results can carry, not about concealment.

## Strongest Counter-Argument

The confirmatory result is a property of arithmetic, and the paper's own tables show it could not have come out otherwise. A fixed-noise Kalman covariance is a near-constant multiple (1.103) of its own mean; the equal-caution sweep removes any constant; so the tested object and the baseline are the same margin. The paper knew this before the fresh runs (tuning runs: 99.57% of rows within 5% of the median, Table S28) and says "a null is expected".

The control meant to rescue the null from being a blunt-instrument null does not do so. The detection oracle E5 uses a ±5% band, yet 99.9% of the tracker's rows lie inside 5% of its median, and the oracle built at the tracker's own spread recovers 0.0001 [0.0000, 0.0002] of the room (Table III), about 130 times less than E5's 0.013. Even E5 is four times smaller than the equivalence margin. No covariance of this amplitude, however well informed, could have left the margin.

The trackers offered as "covariances that vary" do not vary (99.7% within 5%), because the IMM was built from two filters that both fail during braking (NIS about 9 where the room is). The one filter that does vary recovers a significantly positive share, 96% of what its amplitude allows, and is absent from title, abstract and conclusion.

The room itself is mostly not per-decision information: one scale per lead phase recovers 0.916 of it. And in closed loop a plain time gap, with no information about the lead's motion, removes every contact and loses less time than the braking prediction. So the closed loop does not show that "information about the lead's motion" is worth anything; it shows that the class-average baseline is a poor controller.

What remains is a careful protocol applied to a question whose answer was fixed in advance, on one scenario family.

## Ignored Alternative Explanations/Paths

1. **The room is a bias, not a variance.** The planner predicts every actor at constant velocity; the room sits where the tracker's velocity lags a braking lead (lag 1.4-2.5 m, Table III). An oracle "variance" equal to the squared realised error is absorbing a deterministic prediction error. No consistent covariance is meant to cover a model bias of that size, so the question "does the covariance recover the room" may be ill-posed for any covariance, fixed or adaptive. The braking prediction's result is the paper's own evidence for this reading.
2. **The room is between phases, not between decisions.** One scale per estimated lead phase, with no per-decision information, recovers 0.916 of the room, and a single scale by the same rule recovers −0.002 (Table S11). The per-phase rooms on their own frontiers come to about 532 decision-equivalents against 1,204 pooled (my arithmetic from Table S8; the frontiers differ, so this is indicative only). A rank oracle inside a ±5% band cannot reach a room that needs a scale change between phases. The tracker's state estimate, not its covariance, already carries the phase.
3. **Share tracks amplitude.** Across the five trackers the share follows the spread (four at about zero with 99.7-99.9% within 5%; one at 0.046 with 21.5%). The simplest account of every tracker result is amplitude, which is what C1 and M1 ask the paper to state and measure.
4. **Closed-loop gains come from reacting earlier, by any means.** Time gap, per-phase schedule and braking prediction all remove the contacts. The common factor is an earlier, gentler brake (fallback share 2-9% against 53%), not lead-motion information.
5. **The commitment creates the room.** With a 0.75 s reaction and a 6 m/s² stop only 0.101 of the 3 s room remains; under the 3 s commitment the class average has a wrong go in 105 of 105 runs (Table S20) while its closed-loop contact share is 5-20%. The room is largely a property of the no-revision assumption. The paper discloses this; its closed-loop argument for the 3 s reading does not hold (M4).
6. **A stronger baseline was available and not used as one.** The time gap is in the tables as a source. Used as the baseline, it would shrink the room and change every share.

## Missing Stakeholder Perspectives

- Following road users: rear contacts are excluded on the real logs, and no simulated run has traffic behind the ego, although the class average brakes at over 6 m/s².
- Vehicle occupants: deceleration, jerk and fallback share are in the supplement only and enter no classification.
- Tracker and perception designers: an IMM tuned for manoeuvre onset, radar range-rate measurement, or a learned heteroscedastic covariance is not represented.
- Vulnerable road users and crossing traffic: outside the tests, yet Table S28 shows these are the families where the margin ratio varies (4-9% of rows within 5%).
- Safety assessors and regulators: nominal parameters are borrowed from UN R157 with no compliance claim, and no one from that side is asked whether wrong-go share at equal caution is an acceptable safety measure.
- Practitioners deciding whether to keep covariance fields in tracked-object messages: the paper's answer for them ("removing covariance margins is not justified") is not derived from any result in it.

## Unexamined Premise

Equal caution, defined as the ego's own speed given up, is taken as the exchange rate that makes margin sources comparable. Every share, every equivalence and every cost label depends on it. The paper does not argue that this is the cost a planner designer or a road user would accept, and its own closed-loop tables show outcomes (deceleration, fallback share, rest gap) that move strongly at "matched" caution. This is carried as a MAJOR row (M6).

## Observations (Non-Defects)

- The equivalence of the tracker of record with its class average is true and robust: it holds on the grid frontier with a scaled placebo (N minus placebo 0.0002, Table S10), at every update rate and latency (Table S16), in every run set, and under the reaction-and-stop commitment (Table S14). My objection is to what it is said to show, not to the number.
- The IMM result is stable across the plan's transition-matrix settings (Table S6), so it is not an artefact of one diagonal. The objection in M2 is to the two models mixed, not to the mixing.
- The abstract and the contribution list state that cut-in and real logs showed no room and that nothing was tested there. The failed hypotheses are printed with (F).
- The cost of the braking prediction and of the per-phase schedule behind leads that do not brake hard is reported in the main text and the abstract, against the paper's interest.
- The per-phase schedule is correctly marked as not caution-matched and descriptive in closed loop.
- The dependence of the room on the commitment, the update rate and the recording length is disclosed with numbers (Tables V, S10, S14, S16).
- The oracle is described as a reference and not a bound, and the case where a source exceeds it (AM at 1.074 in the stopped phase) is printed.
- The deviations table (S5) is unusually complete, including one error found after the fact.
- The brake test (errors of 1-2%) supports the closed-loop plant model.
- Interval levels for the confirmatory quantities are given with their basis (Table S9); the wider calibrated levels are used where the calibration asked for them.
- The room hypothesis and the per-phase transfer hypothesis could have failed and did not; these are real confirmatory results about the score, although they are not the paper's headline.

## Recommendation Signal

**Major Revision.** Confidence 4 of 5.

The one CRITICAL issue (C1) is repairable by reporting detection at the tracker's own amplitude and rewording; it does not overturn the equivalence. The MAJOR issues are mostly wording and scope, with three that need new values or analyses (M1 amplitude-response curve, M2 a manoeuvre-tuned IMM or a narrower claim, M5 a second baseline and the fallback split). After those changes the paper would claim less than its title does now, and what it claimed would follow from its tables.
