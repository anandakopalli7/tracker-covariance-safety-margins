## The case against publishing as written (one paragraph)

The one result the paper may call "confirmed" for the covariance is a null that could hardly fail: a covariance that is a near-constant multiple of its class average is compared with that class average after the scale has been swept out. The paper says so itself. Everything that makes the paper more than that null sits in three amendments written, locked, run and reported inside about 36 hours (21 Nov 13:30 to 22 Nov 10:00), each one written after the previous results and after an AI panel's criticism, with local hashes as the only evidence of order. In those amendments the covariance gained a little in every lead-braking test in which it varied and there was room (dropouts, replication, 10 Hz, the two-noise IMM, the adaptive filter), yet the abstract opens with "rarely beat ... and then by little". The two controls that pull the gains back down were added last, at a panel's request. A hostile but fair reviewer can therefore say both "the null is trivial" and "the non-null is exploratory", and add that the text, the code, the searches and the review were all done by AI under a single author's direction. What saves the paper is that it hides almost none of this.

## Answers

### 1. Labels and sizes
Right throughout, as far as I traced. "Confirmed" appears only for the original sequence (room, detection, equivalence, IMM class, schedule transfer, braking prediction). Amendment results carry "(A)", "replicated", "failed" or "descriptive" in Table 6, and the text uses "passed; amendment" and "failed; third amendment". Holm is beside the labels and changes none; its scope (six, then two) is stated in IV-E, V-E and the Table 6 caption. Two size problems: the "inside ±5%" reading holds only at start probability 0.10 (DA-5), and "equivalent within 0.10 contact share" reports a resolution limit as a result (DA-7). One label wording: "replicated under rules fixed before the data" is true of the rules; the reader is not told the rules were fixed hours before (DA-4).

### 2. Overreach
- **Dropout gain's source:** overreaches in one clause ("needs no covariance", DA-2). The rest ("its gain over a fixed coast-count margin was not shown", "recovered slightly more") is exact.
- **Closed loop with dropouts:** not overreached. "Might come from a wider margin", "cannot be told from a wider margin", and the calibration miss are all stated. The abstract's "fewer contacts (21% against 33%) but lost 0.18 s more; at equal time lost fewer contacts were not shown" is fair.
- **nuScenes mechanism check:** not overreached; "weakly", "partly a level effect", the training-split choice and the inverse all-pairs sign are in the text. "Consistent with a wider spread around real misses" (VI) is the right strength.
- **Real-log decision test:** not overreached ("nothing was tested there").
- **Two-noise IMM:** "lower bound" is an assertion (DA-9); "not a verdict on maneuver-adaptive covariances" is right.
- **In the null direction:** "rarely beat" (DA-1) and "varies mostly for other reasons" in the abstract (DA-8). This paper's remaining overreach is mostly toward the null, which is the direction its own expectation ("we expect no gain") points.

### 3. Earlier limits
- (a) Closed loop matched only in open-loop caution: **addressed** by the equal-time-lost test, which failed and is reported as failed. Remains: three scales, linear interpolation, one N point, and two of the four arms reused with known results (in A13 and Table S5; the main text says only "the third reuses arms the second had scored").
- (b) Dropout gain possibly a coasting margin: **addressed**, and answered against the paper's interest; one clause now overshoots the other way (DA-2).
- (c) Abstract and conclusion stronger than the body: **mostly addressed**. The universal phrasings are gone. What remains is "rarely" and the pedestrian clause.
- (d) Two-noise IMM's interface: **partly**. Stated as a limit; not tested with a wider interface; "lower bound" unproven.
- (e) Scope of Holm and of the recount: **addressed** in IV-E and Table 6. The acknowledgment still overstates the recount (DA-12).
- (f) Density, round-2 results only in the supplement: **partly**. Table 6 and V-E bring the results in; V-E is 898 words in five long paragraphs with about 40 numbers, and six items still rest on the supplement (Q6).
- Structural: a confirmed result that could hardly fail (**not fixable; admitted**, DA-6); small positive effects (**stated**); one simulator and configuration (**stated; 10 Hz secondary added**); no decision-level real-log evidence (**stated three times**); process (**partly**: local locks and AI authorship are stated; the 36-hour amendment program and its review-driven origin are not, DA-4).

### 4. Readability
The abstract puts the bottom line first, but it is 250 words with 20-odd numbers and a definition of "room" folded into a clause; a reader meets "share of the room" before knowing what a share is. Coined terms are defined once in Table 1. Evidence can be found: each claim in the abstract maps to a row of Table 2, 4 or 6. Three places where it cannot be found without the supplement: what pedestrian crossing is, where the coast-count factors come from, what the primary dropout probability is (Q6). Tables 4 and 5 are typeset on page 8 inside the text of V-E, two sections after they are cited.

### 5. Verdict
- **Recommendation:** major revision.
- **Score:** 5.5 out of 10 (about 3 stars of 5). As the adversarial seat I score what a skeptical referee would defend, not the craft; on craft alone (honest labels, recount, failed controls reported) it would be about 7.
- **Most likely outcome at IEEE OJ-ITS:** a real chance of desk rejection (I would put it near even), on fit and significance (a simulator-only null with small exploratory gains, by an unaffiliated single author) combined with a text and code wholly generated by AI. If it reaches referees: major revision, with a request for real-log decision evidence the author cannot supply quickly.
- **How much of the case survives the paper's disclosures:** less than half. DA-6, 7, 10, 11, 14 are already conceded in the text; a referee who raises them will find the answer on the page. What survives is framing and omission: DA-1, DA-2, DA-5 (the abstract leans to the null), DA-3 and DA-4 (the word in the title, and the unstated speed and origin of the amendments), and DA-12 (one clause of the disclosure).
- **Single change that would raise my score most:** rewrite the first two sentences of the abstract and the first sentence of the conclusion to the symmetric statement (nothing where constant; 2–5% of the room where it varies and there is room; matched by a coast-count margin built from the covariance's own medians; no test possible on cut-in or real logs), and add the one-sentence account of when and why the amendments were written. That needs no new run.
- **Shared-model caveat:** I may share the author-model's taste for hedged, compressed sentences and so under-rate how hard V-E is for a human reader; and I may over-trust "disclosed" as a remedy. A human editor may weigh the AI-authorship and triviality points more heavily than I do.

### 8. AI disclosure
**Does (a), as frozen, meet the rule?** Yes on the three required elements: the system is named (Claude, with the three models; OpenAI Codex), the sections are named (Abstract, Sections I–VII, Supplementary Material; code and figures), and the level is stated plainly ("drafted", "wrote and ran"). It goes beyond the rule by naming the AI review panel. Two faults: it overstates the recount ("every printed value"; IV-E excludes the ‡-marked supplement values), and "made the scientific decisions" is firmer than the deposited amendments support (DA-4).

**Would an editor desk-reject on it?** Not for breaking the disclosure rule; the rule is met. The risk is the fact disclosed: every section drafted by AI, all code written by AI, and a single author. An editor may judge that this exceeds what the journal will accept as authorship, and no wording removes that risk without understating the use. What wording can do is make the human part concrete and checkable. That is the job of the author's own confirmation sentence (a gate), and it is the sentence that matters most: it should list acts (read every section, checked named tables against the result files, approved each amendment before its lock, decided the pedestrian departure), not adjectives.

**(a) against (b).** (b) is shorter, leads with the human role, and its recount sentence is the accurate one ("every value printed in the main text"). But (b) has three faults of its own:
- "made **every** scientific decision" is contradicted by the project's own amendments, which record coordinator (AI) decisions; this is the sentence a reader of the deposit could falsify.
- "sessions that had not seen the first programs" is true, but it drops IV-E's limit that both programs share the scoring library, stored arrays and resample draws; and "audited the claims against the result files" is offered without the pointer to S12.
- It drops two true facts that (a) states: that Claude **ran** the code, and that an **AI panel's reviews shaped the manuscript**. Leaving out the second understates the use, given that all three amendments answer that panel.

**Which lowers the risk without understating:** (b)'s structure with (a)'s completeness. Suggested text (the bracket stays the author's gate):

> "The author conceived the study, set its questions, approved its plans and design decisions, and reviewed and approved all content, and takes full responsibility for the work. [Author's confirmation of what was verified personally.] Generative AI was used and is disclosed here under IEEE policy. Under the author's direction, Claude (Anthropic; models Opus 5.5, Fable 5.2, and Sonnet 5.5) wrote and ran the analysis, simulation-control, and figure code, ran the literature searches, proposed design options for the amendments, and drafted and revised the text of all sections and of the supplementary material. Separate Claude sessions that had not read the first programs recounted every value printed in the main text and all but the marked values of the supplement, using the same scoring library and stored arrays, and audited the claims against the result files (Supplement S12). Pre-submission reviews by a panel of AI reviewers prompted the three amendments; the requests and responses are deposited. OpenAI Codex assisted in developing the simulation, recording, and planner software."

Changes from (b): "made every scientific decision" becomes "approved its plans and design decisions"; "wrote" becomes "wrote and ran"; the recount scope and its shared components are stated as in IV-E; the AI panel is restored; "proposed design options" is added so that the deposit and the paragraph agree.
