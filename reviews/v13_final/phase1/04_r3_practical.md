## Answers

### 1. Labels and sizes
Mostly right. Section V-A uses "confirmed" only for the original plan (room, E5 detection, tracker equivalence, IMM classification, per-phase transfer, CK3 above 0.5) and marks the tracker of record "amplitude-limited (descriptive)", with "detection at its spread is not shown": at true size. The amendment tests carry "passed (amendment)", "replicated" or "failed", and Table 6 distinguishes "(A)", "failed", "descriptive", "secondary". The two third-amendment controls are labelled "failed" and the text says the dropout gain "is thus matched by a coasting margin". Sizes are given: 2.8%, 3.0%, 2.2% "inside the paper's ±5% equivalence margin ... (no equivalence test was registered for them)". Two problems: the IV-E Holm sentence (R3-1) mislabels which hypotheses the Holm set holds, and the Holm result for the two third-amendment controls is not in the main text (R3-7). The statement that Holm "changes none" is made for the six (V-E last paragraph) but not for the third pair. The closed-loop "fewer contacts" is correctly qualified "(passed; amendment)" and then "But ... the more cautious one".

### 2. Overreach
- Dropout gain's source: handled. The text says it is "matched by a coasting margin, a level effect that needs no covariance", and "ranking among dropout decisions is not shown at 2 Hz". The abstract still lists "it gains a little where it grows during dropouts" before the coast-count caveat; acceptable.
- Closed loop: handled. "Matched in open-loop caution", then "might come from a wider margin", then "failed; third amendment". The abstract ends the thread ("fewer contacts were not shown"). Residual: the cost is read on the easy cases only (R3-2).
- nuScenes mechanism check: handled (weak, inverse over all, level effect, primary population chosen on training split).
- Real-log decision test: "no room", "nothing was tested there", so no claim.
- Two-noise IMM: handled ("lower bound for that interface, not a verdict on maneuver-adaptive covariances"). One unfair-looking sentence remains in V-E: "a gain from level or shape ... that follows the phase" asserts a mechanism for a share the paper calls detectable but small and un-tested for equivalence; it is labelled descriptive, so tolerable.
- Remaining soft spots: Section VII "a plain time gap with less" is descriptive at one level; the conclusion says "recovered most of the pooled room" for CK3, which is open loop with a cost in progress stated in the same sentence.

### 3. Earlier limits
(a) Closed loop matched only in open-loop caution: partly addressed. The equal-time-lost comparison was run (failed) and the text no longer claims more. Remaining: it is one frontier point interpolated between scales 4 and 8 (S15), on a time-lost measure that exists only for non-contact cases (R3-2).
(b) Dropout gain vs coast margin: addressed. The coast-count control matched or beat it; the wording changed to a level effect. Remaining: random masks only (R3-4).
(c) Abstract and conclusion stronger than the body: addressed. "Only where it varies", "where it tracks the error" and "shows on real data" are gone; the abstract's claims match Tables 2, 4, 6.
(d) Two-noise IMM interface: partly addressed. The wording is honest; the interface limit (position-velocity block, constant-velocity prediction) is unchanged by design, so the verdict on maneuver-adaptive covariances stays open.
(e) Holm and recount scope: partly addressed. IV-E now says sequences are not adjusted for one another and that second programs recount every main-text value and all but marked supplement values and share the library, arrays and draws. The Holm sentence is ambiguous (R3-1); the third pair's Holm value is missing from the main text.
(f) Density, round-2 results only in the supplement: partly addressed. Tables 5 and 6, Fig. 4 and the dropout, IMM, closed-loop and nuScenes numbers are now in the main text. The cost is length: body about 6,270 words, 12 typeset pages (references end on page 12), against a target near 10; Section V-E holds one 898-word block.
Structural limits stay as the prior panel judged: a confirmed result that could hardly fail (near-constant covariance), small positive effects, one simulator and configuration, no decision-level real-log evidence, and the process (amendments chosen after results, local hash locks, AI authorship). Wording cannot fix these and the paper no longer tries to.

### 4. Readability
Bottom line is first in the abstract. Coined terms are defined once in Table 1 and Sections IV-B and IV-C ("room", "share", "equal caution", "recovers", "uninformative variation"). Every claim's evidence is findable in a main-text table. Remaining gaps: "time lost" and "rest gap" are never defined in the main text (Q6 case 2); "CANMOT" and "CARLA" are not expanded at first use (names; the reference gives the expansion for CANMOT); and the abstract and Section V-E are very dense. The three-sentence summary a safety engineer wants exists only in Section VI's last sentences ("None of this justifies removing a covariance margin from a deployed stack: young and coasting tracks, other road users, and other sensors can make a covariance vary").

### 5. Verdict
Recommendation: major revision (a light one: the text is honest and well-labelled, the remaining changes are a few edits and definitions). Score: 6.8 of 10; 3.5 stars. Most likely editorial outcome at IEEE OJ-ITS: major revision, with the reviewers' main objections being one simulator and configuration, effects that are small or null, an unusually dense statistical apparatus for a regular paper, and 12 pages. The single change that would raise my score most: bring into the main text, with their definitions, the closed-loop cost quantities (time lost baseline, the share of cases it covers, who bears it), and state the practical reading in two plain sentences in the abstract: a plain time gap or a braking prediction removed contacts in this test bed where the covariance margin did not, and none of this licenses removing a covariance margin.

### 8. AI disclosure
IEEE's rule needs: system named, sections that use it, level of use, in the acknowledgments. Version (a), as frozen, meets all three: it names Claude (Anthropic) and each model, says it "carried out the literature searches and summaries, wrote and ran the analysis, simulation-control, and figure code, and drafted the Abstract, Sections I-VII, and the Supplementary Material", names Codex for the software, and says that an AI panel's pre-submission reviews shaped the text. A desk-reject on it is unlikely: the disclosure is more complete than most. Two weaknesses: it opens with a sentence fragment ("Content generated by artificial intelligence (AI), disclosed under IEEE policy.") and it is long and model-by-model, so an editor must work to find the level of use; and "second programs that recount every printed value" overstates scope against Section IV-E, which says every value printed in the main text and all but the marked supplement values, with a shared scoring library, arrays and draws.
Version (b), the candidate: shorter, leads with author responsibility, and states the scope of the recount correctly ("every value printed in the main text"), which matches Section IV-E. But it understates in three places: (i) it drops the AI panel sentence ("Pre-submission reviews by an AI panel shaped this manuscript"), which is a real use (the supplement records that the third amendment's two controls and the coast-count baseline were asked for by panel seats); (ii) "made every scientific decision" is stronger than the record, which has panel-proposed amendments the author accepted; (iii) "all sections" leaves the Abstract unnamed.
Recommendation: use (b) as the base with three edits: keep (a)'s AI-panel sentence; change "made every scientific decision" to "made the scientific decisions and accepted or declined each AI-proposed change"; name the Abstract ("the Abstract, all sections, and the supplementary material") and add a plain level-of-use clause ("substantially all of the text and all of the code were produced by the AI systems"). Also state that the recount shares the scoring library, arrays and draws, as IV-E does, or drop "separate". This lowers desk-reject risk (shorter, responsibility first, scope matching IV-E) without understating. The author's own confirmation sentence is a gate and is not judged.
