## The five answers

**1. True size and labels.** Mostly yes. "Confirmed" is used only for the original plan, and amendments say "passed (amendment)" or "replicated under rules fixed before the data". Holm is reported beside the six primaries. Sizes are small and stated (2.8-3.0% of the room; 2.2% for the two-noise IMM). Two gaps: the closed-loop contact reduction (21% vs 33%) is reported at a size that the open-loop shares (about 3%) do not explain, and nothing reconciles them (finding 1); and the closed-loop equivalence margin (plus or minus 0.10) is wide next to the effects the paper calls gains (finding 4).

**2. Claims beyond tests.** (a) Dropout gain's source: handled well in V-E (substitution, not ranking); the abstract and conclusion do not carry it, and the untested fixed-increase rule is the obvious rival (finding 6). (b) Caution-matched closed loop: the matched caution sits at the bottom of the grid and the baseline's contact share doubled from tuning to test; abstract and conclusion print the result without these caveats (findings 1, 2). (c) nuScenes mechanism check: the population choice is disclosed, and tau is about 0.11, which is a weak ranking; fine as worded, but it is cited as support for real dropouts in a way the data do not carry (finding 3). (d) Real-log decision test with no room: worded correctly ("nothing was tested"), and the "contacts from behind not counted" change is disclosed.

**3. Previous limits.** Near-constant covariance (headline hard to fail): partly addressed; the paper now shows where it varies (dropouts, IMM, pedestrian) and reports a sensitivity control, but the varying-covariance gains are small and synthetic. Thin evidence where covariance varies: partly; replication on 150 new runs and a dose-response help, remaining gap is real data. Same-day amendments without multiplicity control: largely addressed for the second amendment (Holm, six hypotheses); the first amendment and the sequences are still not adjusted jointly (disclosed). One configuration at 2 Hz without latency: partly; a 10 Hz, 0.1 s secondary configuration agrees, outside the Holm family. Unfair IMM arm: largely addressed by the two-noise IMM (2.2% of its room, "passed (amendment)"). Closed loop not caution-matched: partly; matched in open loop only, baseline at grid bottom, one level, lead braking only (findings 1, 5, 6).

**4. Readability.** The abstract does not put the bottom line first and stacks eight or more numbers in one paragraph; terms are defined once in Table I but the abstract uses them before it. Evidence for each claim is findable through table and supplement pointers (good). The conclusion repeats the abstract's uncaveated closed-loop sentence.

**5. Recommendation.** **Major revision.** Score **6.5/10** (about 3.5 stars). Most likely outcome at IEEE OJ-ITS: major revision, with a request for a stronger closed-loop comparison and a plain statement of scope. Single change that would raise my score most: a closed-loop frontier for the dropout loop (class average and covariance at several scales, plus a fixed or miss-counter margin arm and a time-gap arm), so that "fewer contacts for 0.18 s" is shown against the cheapest alternatives and not against a class average at the bottom of its grid.
