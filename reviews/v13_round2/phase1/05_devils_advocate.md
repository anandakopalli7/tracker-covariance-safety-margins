## The case against, in one paragraph

What a hostile but fair reviewer writes first: "The one result that carries the word *confirmed* is an algebraic near-tautology (a near-constant multiple of the class average, under a sweep that removes scale, equals the class average), which the paper now admits. Every positive result is an amendment test designed, run, analysed and written within about a day, after the confirmatory data were seen, in response to review scores. Those positive results are each smaller than the paper's own ±5% equivalence margin, with one exception, a closed loop whose 'caution match' was made in open loop on other runs and whose own outcomes show the covariance arm stopped a metre further back. And the paper's own decomposition says the dropout gain is the extra margin that coasting decisions get, which a one-line rule (widen the margin while a track is unmatched) would give without any covariance; that rule was not tested. So the thesis 'a covariance helps where it tracks the error' has not been separated from 'a wider margin during coasting helps'."

How much survives the paper's disclosures: about half. The paper states nearly every one of these facts itself, and its labels are honest. What survives is (i) the emphasis and wording of the closed-loop result in the abstract and conclusion, (ii) the unstated fact that the "recovers" results sit inside the paper's own equivalence margin, and (iii) the untested coasting-flag baseline, which is a missing control, not a wording matter.

## What was fixed before the data and what was chosen after (from the amendments)

- **Fixed before its data (holds up):** the six Holm primaries (AMENDMENT_12 §4, under the first lock); the dropout replication's mask law, output rule and class; the dose-response slope; the two-noise IMM's design and tuning rule; the closed loop's match table for all four candidate levels, computed before any calibration run; the mechanism check's gate, strata and horizon.
- **Chosen after seeing related results (all disclosed):** dropouts and the twin test themselves (first amendment, after the fresh results); the mechanism check's primary population (on train, after the planned statistic was seen to fail there, with power for validation estimated at about 0.002); the IMM quiet mode (on tuning runs); the closed-loop arm's scale, matched on runs whose dropout results were known; the rear-contact rule on nuScenes; testing pedestrian crossing despite its own no-go.
- **Motive:** the second amendment's approval record states that its aim was to remove a review panel's blockers within a 12-hour window. The rules were locked before each data set, but the choice of *which* tests to add was outcome-aware. The paper says the reviews "shaped this manuscript"; I judge that sufficient.
- I found no label in the manuscript stronger than the amendment record supports. "Confirmed" is used only for the original plan; amendment results carry "(passed; amendment)" or "replicated under rules fixed before the data".

## Answers to the five questions

**1. True size and right label?** Labels: yes, throughout. Sizes: the numbers match between text and supplement where I traced them, but two are framed larger than they are. The closed-loop contact difference is presented as a covariance effect at equal caution when the closed-loop outcomes show unequal caution (finding 1). The "recovers" shares are not placed against the paper's own ±5% margin (finding 3). Holm is reported beside the six round-2 primaries (V-E, Table S38).

**2. Does any sentence claim more than its test allows?**
- *Dropout gain's source:* V-E is honest (extra margin, not ranking; fixed increase untested). The abstract and Section VI are not aligned with it: "where it tracks the error, it helps" reads as information value (finding 2).
- *Caution-matched closed loop:* over-claimed in the abstract and conclusion (finding 1). V-E discloses the grid bottom and the open-loop match, but not the rest-gap evidence or the "wider margin" reading.
- *nuScenes mechanism check:* the population choice is disclosed; the negative all-pairs result is understated (finding 6); "ranks the realised error" is partly a missed-against-not-missed contrast (finding 2).
- *Real-log decision test:* stated correctly as no room and nothing tested, in the abstract, V-E and VII.

**3. The previous round's limits.**
- *Headline equivalence could hardly fail:* addressed by admission, not by evidence. It remains the only confirmed covariance result.
- *Thin evidence where the covariance varies:* partly addressed. Replication, dose-response and a second configuration are real gains. Remaining: effects inside ±5%, one synthetic mask law, no coasting-flag control.
- *Same-day amendments without multiplicity control:* partly addressed. Holm over six; the second amendment is itself a same-day amendment. Rules-before-data holds.
- *One configuration at 2 Hz, no latency:* partly addressed (dropouts at 10 Hz with latency, secondary). Still one simulator, detector, planner and road.
- *Unfair IMM arm:* addressed. The two-noise IMM is a fair arm and recovers 2.2%.
- *Closed loop not caution-matched:* partly addressed. Matched in open loop only; closed-loop caution visibly differs.

**4. Readability.** The abstract opens with the bottom line and is dense but parseable; it carries about fourteen numbers, and the parenthesis "(lower level; class-average contacts 20%)" cannot be understood before Section V-C. Coined terms are defined once in Table I. Evidence was findable for every claim I traced. One snag: "the room" changes between tests (5.58, 5.63, 5.21, 4.95, 0.16 points) and shares of different rooms are quoted side by side as if comparable.

**5. Recommendation.** **Major revision** (text changes for findings 1, 3, 4, 5, 6; one small post hoc analysis for finding 2). **Score 6/10, three stars of five.** Most likely outcome at IEEE OJ-ITS: major revision, with a real chance of rejection from a referee who weighs the near-tautological confirmed result and the one-day amendments heavily. The single change that would raise my score most: score the coasting-flag margin (class average widened by a constant while the track is unmatched) against the covariance on the stored dropout arrays; if the covariance does not beat it, say so and restate the positive finding as "a margin that widens during coasting helps". With that and the closed-loop rewording I would give 7.
