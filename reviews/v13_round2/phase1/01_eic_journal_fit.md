## The five answers

**1. Is each result reported at its true size, with its label right?** Mostly in the body, less in the front matter. In the body, "confirmed" is used only for the original locked sequence and "passed (amendment)" for the later tests; the dropout replication is marked "replicated under rules fixed before the data"; the adaptive-filter twin is marked "passed; specified post hoc"; sizes are small and given with intervals (2.8%, 3.0%, 2.2%); Holm over the six round-2 primaries is stated and changes no label (Table S38). Gaps: the abstract drops the caveats that make the closed-loop and dropout gains modest (finding 2, 3); "preregistered" in the title is stronger than local-hash locks (finding 5); Holm is one prose sentence, not beside the results (findings 1, 9); "passed" on null results reads as success (finding 8).

**2. Does any sentence claim more than its test allows?** Four do. (a) The dropout gain: the thesis "helps where it tracks the error" is not what the within-dropout share (0.009 [-0.008, 0.024]) shows; the body admits the gain is extra margin, the abstract and conclusion do not. (b) Caution-matched closed loop: matched in open loop, class average at the bottom of the grid (17% contact there), one level, a wider fixed dropout margin untested; the abstract says "caution-matched" without these. (c) nuScenes mechanism check: "as the nuScenes check shows on real data" (Section VI) rests on tau 0.108 from a population chosen on the training split, with all-pairs tau negative. (d) The real-log decision test that found no room is reported honestly everywhere ("nothing was tested"), which is correct; the "no room" outcome is a coverage limit, not support, and the paper says so.

**3. Previous-round limits.**
- *Headline equivalence could hardly fail (near-constant covariance):* partly addressed. The paper now says so openly ("as the constancy of its margin led one to expect") and adds tests where the covariance varies; the confirmatory result is still a near-tautology and the paper cannot change that. Remains: its value as a headline.
- *Thin evidence where covariance varies:* partly addressed. Dropouts, a replication on 150 new runs, a dose-response, a two-noise IMM, pedestrian crossing and a nuScenes check were added; all gains are 2-3% of room, one mechanism (more margin during dropouts) is untested against a fixed wider margin, and real-log decisions still have no room.
- *Same-day amendments without multiplicity control:* partly addressed. A Holm table over the second amendment's six primaries is added; the first amendment and the first-round sequences are "not adjusted for one another"; the second amendment's tests were written after fresh results were seen, and locks are local hashes.
- *One configuration at 2 Hz with no latency:* partly addressed. A 10 Hz, 0.1 s-latency run is reported (Table V; S15), as secondary; main claims remain at 2 Hz and the abstract says "Magnitudes hold in one simulated configuration at 2 Hz".
- *Unfair IMM arm:* addressed in substance: a re-tuned IMM and a two-noise IMM were added, the latter recovering 2.2%; the registered classification is kept as the confirmatory result, which is honest.
- *Closed loop not caution-matched:* partly addressed. A caution-matched closed loop with dropouts is added (21% vs 33%); the match is open-loop and the comparison arm sits at the grid floor. The non-dropout closed loop (Table IV) still has one unmatched arm (PS) labelled descriptive.

**4. Readability.** Below the standard for a journal readership. The bottom line is not first in the abstract (finding 6); one paragraph carries about 25 numbers; terms are defined once in Table I, which is good, but there are too many and several are used once. A reader can find each round-1 claim's evidence in Tables II-V, but cannot find the round-2 evidence without a table (finding 1) and must go to Supplement S15 for Holm. Section V-E, 774 words, is the densest in the paper. Length is at the cap with no slack (finding 13).

**5. Recommendation.** **Major revision.** **Score 6 of 10 (3 stars out of 5).** Likely editorial outcome at IEEE OJ-ITS: major revision after editorial screening; the risk to acceptance is significance (a null in one simulated configuration, with small positives) and the AI-authorship questions at the integrity check, not method. A desk reject is possible if the associate editor reads the abstract alone and finds the framing inflated. Single change that would raise my score most: add one main-text table of the round-2 results with labels, sizes and Holm-adjusted p, and rewrite the abstract to lead with the verdict and to carry the three caveats of findings 2-3 (matched in open loop, class average at the grid floor, gain from extra margin not from ranking). That addresses findings 1-3 and 6 together, and lifts the paper to about 7.
