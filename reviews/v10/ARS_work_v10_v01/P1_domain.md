## Contract Paraphrase

D1 (methodology_rigor). Seen from the domain-accuracy chair, this dimension asks whether the experimental machinery would satisfy a careful referee in this field: how the runs are designed, how data, seeds and episodes are managed, how outcomes are summarised and tested statistically, and whether an independent group could rerun the study. It is mandatory and belongs to the methodology seat, so I do not plan a score for it. It matters to me only because a faithful reading of the literature cannot rescue an evaluation whose numbers cannot be reproduced or whose statistics are under-reported.

D2 (domain_accuracy). This is my dimension. It asks whether what the work asserts agrees with what the field currently knows. Earlier contributions must be described faithfully and credited to the right sources. Technical vocabulary must keep its accepted meaning, for example estimator consistency versus accuracy, covariance versus realised error, probabilistic (chance) constraints versus worst-case margins, and time-to-collision, headway and string-stability notions. Any result, property or theorem imported from the estimation, planning or vehicle-control literature must be stated correctly. Because it is mandatory, a fatal finding here would on its own push the panel towards rejection under the contract's failure rules, and a block finding would push it towards major revision.

D3 (argumentative_coherence). This dimension asks whether the central thesis holds together. The chain from evidence to conclusion should have no gaps or fallacies, and no premise that carries weight should simply be asserted, including assumptions about which road users, operators or other stakeholders gain, which bear the cost, and which are left out. It is mandatory and owned by the devil's-advocate seat, with the methodology seat also eligible. I do not score it and would mention it only where a coherence failure comes from a domain misreading.

D4 (cross_disciplinary_relevance). This dimension asks whether readers from neighbouring communities, such as control, state estimation, traffic-flow theory, human factors and safety assurance, can follow the framing and definitions, and whether any claim that crosses a disciplinary boundary is actually backed by evidence. It is high priority and belongs to the perspective seat. It is outside what I score.

D5 (writing_and_structure). This dimension covers how well the manuscript is organised and explained, whether its figures and tables do their job, and whether its formatting follows the conventions its target outlet expects. It has normal priority and belongs to the editor-in-chief seat. I do not score it.

D6 (venue_fit_and_contribution). This dimension asks whether the work belongs in the configured outlet and gives that outlet's readers something new and worthwhile. It is mandatory and owned by the editor-in-chief seat. My D2 judgement of whether prior work is represented correctly feeds into that novelty assessment but does not replace it. I make no claim about it in this phase.

## Scoring Plan

### D2: domain_accuracy
dimension_id: D2
what_to_look_for: Faithful description and correct attribution of prior work along the perception-tracking-planning chain (Kalman and multiple-model/IMM tracking, filter consistency and covariance realism checks such as NEES/NIS, propagation of state uncertainty into prediction and planning, chance-constrained and risk-aware speed or trajectory planning, robust and safety-filter alternatives such as RSS-style rules or control barrier functions, adaptive cruise control and car-following models such as IDM, and existing studies that judge perception or tracking quality by downstream decision outcomes rather than estimation error); domain terms used in their accepted sense; imported theory and results stated correctly; comparators that reflect current practice and are tuned fairly; test-bed scenarios, sensor, noise and latency models representative of the operating domain the conclusions address; and conclusions scoped to what the domain evidence can carry.
what_triggers_block: A correctable but material domain defect touching the central claim: a prior work misrepresented or misattributed on a point the contribution depends on (for instance asserting a research gap that existing decision-level or risk-aware planning studies already cover); omission of a core literature strand that bears directly on the research question; misuse of a central concept (for instance treating a filter's reported covariance as calibrated without any consistency evidence, or equating a probabilistic constraint with a worst-case guarantee); comparators that are weak, mistuned or unrepresentative of standard practice so that a reported advantage cannot be interpreted; or generalisation from a narrow simulated test bed (one simulator, one scenario family, idealised sensing or noise) to claims about automated driving in general.
what_triggers_warn: Lesser domain gaps that do not carry the central claim: incomplete or dated coverage of a secondary literature strand; loose or inconsistent terminology that leaves conclusions unchanged; a reasonable baseline or comparator whose tuning or parameter provenance is under-documented; test-bed representativeness limits that are acknowledged but not quantified (noise model, sensor latency, scenario parameter ranges); or wording slightly broader than the evidence where a scoping sentence would resolve it.
what_triggers_fatal: A domain error that invalidates the central conclusion and cannot be repaired by rewriting: the core argument rests on a factually wrong statement of established estimation, uncertainty-propagation or planning theory that the evaluation logic depends on; the claimed contribution is already established in prior work that is omitted or misrepresented, leaving no novel domain finding; or the evaluation setting is so mis-specified relative to the domain that the uncertainty under study is absent or trivially fixed by construction, so the results cannot address the stated question.
