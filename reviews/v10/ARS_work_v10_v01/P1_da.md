## Contract Paraphrase

D1 (methodology_rigor; mandatory; owned by the methodology seat). Read adversarially, this dimension asks whether the study design, the handling of data, the statistical reporting and the support for reproduction would survive a hostile methods referee in this field: could a sceptic rerun the work, would the reported numbers hold under other seeds, splits or equally reasonable analytic choices, and are uncertainty and sample sizes reported well enough to tell a claimed difference apart from noise. I do not score it, but any methodological weakness that a core claim leans on is legitimate material for my challenges.

D2 (domain_accuracy; mandatory; owned by the domain seat). This asks whether what the manuscript says about the state of the field, about earlier results and about technical vocabulary matches current evidence. The adversarial version is whether earlier work is misstated or selectively cited so as to manufacture a gap, or whether a term is used in a non-standard sense that silently changes what a claim means. I do not score it; misrepresented prior work that props up the novelty or the central argument will be raised as a challenge.

D3 (argumentative_coherence; mandatory; owned by the Devil's Advocate seat and also eligible for the methodology seat). This is the one dimension I score. It asks whether the central thesis holds together as an argument: whether the evidence actually presented reaches the headline claims at the strength and breadth at which they are stated, whether the reasoning avoids fallacies such as circularity, equivocation or affirming the consequent, and whether every premise a core claim relies on is supported, explicitly including premises about who gains from the proposal, who pays for it and who or what is excluded from consideration. My adversarial stance is to build the strongest rival explanation for each key result, to look for conclusions stretched beyond the conditions actually tested, and to check whether analysis choices that determine the outcome were fixed in advance or made after the results were seen.

D4 (cross_disciplinary_relevance; high priority; owned by the perspective seat). This asks whether a reader from a neighbouring discipline could follow the framing, the definitions and the implications, and whether claims that reach into other disciplines are backed rather than merely asserted. The adversarial reading is whether broad statements of relevance to other communities are earned or decorative. It is not mine to score; an unsupported interdisciplinary implication on which the thesis depends would feed a D3 challenge.

D5 (writing_and_structure; normal priority; owned by the editor-in-chief seat). This covers organisation, clarity of exposition, the quality of figures and tables, and adherence to the conventions of the venue. The adversarial angle is whether presentation hides something, for instance a qualification placed far from the claim it limits, or a visual or tabular layout that makes a weak effect look strong. It is not mine to score; where presentation masks an argumentative gap I will raise the gap itself as a coherence challenge.

D6 (venue_fit_and_contribution; mandatory; owned by the editor-in-chief seat). This asks whether the manuscript suits the configured venue and makes an original, significant contribution for that readership. Adversarially, the question is whether a sceptical editor would judge the contribution incremental, already known, or smaller than advertised once any overstatement is stripped away. It is not mine to score, and because this call carries no criteria binding I make no claim about alignment with any venue; but if the claimed contribution rests on reasoning that fails under D3 scrutiny, that dependency will appear among my challenges.

## Scoring Plan

### D3: argumentative_coherence
dimension_id: D3
what_to_look_for: Map every headline claim (title, abstract, stated contributions, conclusion) to the specific evidence offered for it and test whether that evidence reaches the claim at its stated strength and scope; enumerate each premise a core claim depends on, including assumptions about who benefits, who bears the cost and who is left out, and check that each is supported; construct the strongest alternative explanation for each key result and check whether the manuscript rules it out; check for circularity (the conclusion built into the measure, the comparator or the evaluation setup), equivocation on key terms, generalisation beyond the conditions actually tested, and conclusions that hinge on thresholds, metrics, subsets or baselines that appear to have been chosen or tuned after seeing results.
what_triggers_block: At least one headline claim is stated more strongly or more broadly than its evidence allows, or depends on an unsupported premise (including an unexamined assumption about who benefits, who bears the cost or who is left out), or a plausible rival explanation for a key result is neither ruled out nor addressed, or a key result depends on an outcome-determining analysis choice that looks post hoc and has no sensitivity check; the thesis remains salvageable through added analysis, reframing or substantially narrowed claims.
what_triggers_warn: One or more secondary claims or stated implications extend beyond the tested conditions or rest on an unstated premise, or a plausible alternative explanation is left undiscussed, while the headline claims stay supported at their stated strength and each gap can be closed by narrowing wording, adding an explicit limitation or acknowledging the alternative without new analysis.
what_triggers_fatal: The central thesis is unsupported by the manuscript's own evidence even after reasonable narrowing, because that evidence contradicts it or fits a rival explanation equally well, or because the argument is circular so that the conclusion is guaranteed by how the measure or comparison was constructed, or because the thesis is internally inconsistent across sections, or because the core claim rests wholly on an unsupported premise or post-hoc choice whose removal leaves no supported contribution.
