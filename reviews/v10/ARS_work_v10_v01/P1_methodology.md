## Contract Paraphrase

D1 (methodology_rigor). From my seat this is the core question: can the stated design actually answer the stated question, and would an independent group get the same numbers with the same procedure? I read it as requiring a design whose conditions differ only in the factor being tested, an explicit unit of independent replication (run, seed, or scenario instance) at which variance is estimated and resampling is performed, interval methods that are valid for the specific quantity being summarised (including non-smooth summaries such as extremes, quantiles, or rare-event rates), honest handling of multiplicity, a visible line between analyses fixed in advance and those chosen after seeing results, and enough released detail (configurations, seeds, run counts, software versions, code or logs) to rerun the evaluation.

D2 (domain_accuracy). This dimension asks whether the work is correct about its own field: whether statements about established results and prior methods are accurate, whether terminology is used as the field uses it, and whether the paper's results are positioned truthfully against what is already known. I do not score it, but from a methodology viewpoint it matters wherever a baseline, metric definition, or simulator convention is borrowed from prior work, because a misdescribed baseline or a misused metric changes what the comparison means.

D3 (argumentative_coherence). I read this as the bridge between evidence and conclusion: the central thesis must hold together, each headline claim must be licensed by the evidence actually reported (direction, size, and uncertainty), the reasoning must not rest on a fallacy or a circular definition, and any premise a core claim needs must itself be supported, including premises about which users, scenarios, or stakeholders gain from the proposed approach and which absorb its costs or are not covered. From a statistical standpoint this is where I check that wording in the abstract and conclusions matches the reported intervals and the scope of the conditions tested.

D4 (cross_disciplinary_relevance). This dimension concerns whether readers from neighbouring fields can follow the framing, definitions, and implications, and whether any claim that reaches into another discipline is backed up rather than asserted. I do not score it; methodologically it bears on whether evaluation terms (for example, what counts as a primary outcome versus an intermediate one) are defined precisely enough for an outside reader to interpret the results without ambiguity.

D5 (writing_and_structure). This dimension covers how the manuscript is organised, how clearly it explains itself, whether its figures and tables are informative and well built, and whether it follows the venue's conventions. I do not score it, but I note that statistical transparency often lives or dies in tables and figure captions: whether intervals, sample sizes, and the resampling unit are shown next to the point estimates they qualify.

D6 (venue_fit_and_contribution). This dimension asks whether the work belongs in the configured venue and whether it adds something new and meaningful for that readership. I do not score it and make no venue judgement here; from my seat the only link is that a contribution claim is only as strong as the evaluation that supports it, which is assessed under D1 and D3.

## Scoring Plan

### D1: methodology_rigor
dimension_id: D1
what_to_look_for: A design in which compared conditions differ only in the manipulated factor (shared scenarios, seeds, or common random numbers, analysed as paired when paired); a stated unit of independent replication with variance estimation and bootstrap resampling done at that unit (run- or cluster-level, not pooled timesteps or frames); interval methods named and justified for each reported functional (percentile, BCa, or studentized bootstrap with replicate count stated; exact or score intervals for rare-event proportions; methods valid for non-smooth summaries such as minima, maxima, or tail quantiles); run counts justified or at least reported per condition; multiplicity across metrics, scenarios, and parameter settings either controlled or explicitly declared; a clear separation of pre-specified primary analyses from exploratory or post-hoc ones, including how thresholds, metrics, and exclusions were fixed; for any counterfactual or off-policy estimate, stated support or overlap and estimator assumptions; sensitivity analyses for key tuning choices; and reproducibility artefacts (code, configs, seeds, simulator and software versions, raw per-run outputs).
what_triggers_block: A material but correctable analysis defect affects a headline result: intervals or tests treat within-run observations as independent or never state the resampling unit; the main comparison is reported without any uncertainty quantification; paired designs are analysed as unpaired (or vice versa) in a way that changes conclusions; an interval method is used that is known to be invalid for the functional reported (for example, a naive bootstrap for an extreme-value summary or a normal approximation for near-zero event rates) with no justification; many metric-by-condition comparisons are reported with headline claims resting on individually significant ones and no multiplicity handling or declaration; key thresholds or metric definitions appear chosen after seeing results with no sensitivity check; or the reported procedure lacks the seeds, run counts, or configuration detail needed to rerun it.
what_triggers_warn: Analysis is sound in substance but under-documented or modestly limited: the bootstrap replicate count or interval type is not stated; run counts per condition are small but acknowledged as a limitation; exploratory analyses exist and are labelled as such but are not clearly separated in presentation; code or configuration release is promised but not yet available; an approximate interval method is used where a better one exists but is unlikely to change any conclusion; or a secondary metric lacks a sensitivity analysis.
what_triggers_fatal: The design cannot answer its own question even after reanalysis: the compared conditions differ in more than the manipulated factor so the effect is confounded by construction; the decision thresholds, tuning, or metric choices were optimised on the same runs used to report the headline effect with no held-out or pre-registered evaluation; a counterfactual or off-policy estimate is reported for actions outside the support of the logged data; reported counts, sample sizes, or intervals are arithmetically impossible or mutually inconsistent in a way that undermines the main result; or once dependence is handled at the correct resampling unit no supported headline claim remains.

### D3: argumentative_coherence
dimension_id: D3
what_to_look_for: Whether each claim in the abstract, contributions list, and conclusions follows from the reported intervals in direction, magnitude, and certainty; whether claims of no difference or equivalence rest on a stated equivalence or non-inferiority margin rather than on a non-significant result; whether practical significance is argued separately from statistical significance; whether generalisation is limited to the conditions, scenarios, and parameter ranges actually tested; whether causal or mechanistic language matches what the design can identify; whether the stated hypotheses or research questions map onto the analyses performed; whether the evaluation metric is defined independently of the conclusion (no circularity); and whether premises about who benefits, who bears the cost, or which cases are left out are supported by evidence rather than asserted.
what_triggers_block: A headline claim outruns its evidence but a narrower claim is still supportable: an improvement is asserted when the interval includes zero or only practically negligible effects; equivalence or harmlessness is inferred from non-significance; results from a limited set of conditions are generalised in the abstract or conclusions to settings not tested; causal language is used where only association or a single configuration was examined; or a premise the central claim relies on (for example, about which users or scenarios benefit and which absorb the cost) is stated without supporting evidence.
what_triggers_warn: The argument is coherent overall but has localised slippage: discussion-section wording is stronger than the corresponding interval without affecting the headline; limitations are acknowledged in one section but not carried into the conclusions; a secondary hypothesis is stated but its test is only partly reported; or trade-offs between outcomes are mentioned but not quantified.
what_triggers_fatal: The central thesis is contradicted or rendered empty by the paper's own reported results: the reported intervals exclude the claimed effect or point in the opposite direction; the main conclusion holds by construction because the evaluation metric or comparison was defined to guarantee it; or the thesis depends on an unsupported premise without which no core claim survives.
