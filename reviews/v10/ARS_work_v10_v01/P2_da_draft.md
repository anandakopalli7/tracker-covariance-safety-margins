### D3 Score Basis
Block, repairable. The block trigger is met by a headline claim of contribution 3 and Section VII: that the decision value the paper could measure lies in anticipating braking. That location depends on premises the paper does not support. The first is that the cross-phase remainder reflects anticipating braking rather than single-scale pooling and recording length (M1). The second is that the prior's cost outside braking does not matter (M2). The third is that equal caution means equal cost (M3). The fourth is that the prior's open-loop, off-policy gain carries over to the states its own planner would visit (M6). The room against which the covariance's share is stated is not shown to be one a covariance can use (M4), and the commitment bracket in the abstract rests on a truncation convention (M5). The covariance null survives all six, so the thesis can be repaired by added analyses that the existing recordings and simulator support, or by substantially narrowed claims. No single defect meets the CRITICAL bar, so the CRITICAL table is empty by judgement, not by omission.

### What the Paper Does Well
The covariance null is robust. It holds under all 21 scorings (Table S1) and in the estimation-level ranking check (Section V-D), and Fig. 2(b) explains it with a mechanism the paper demonstrates rather than asserts. The paper fixes claim wording before computing values, uses two independent programs, reports effects in decisions as well as shares, and tests its own measure with graded oracles. That last check is what exposes several of the weaknesses below.

### Strongest Counter-Argument
The title question is settled before any decision is scored. CANMOT's fixed noise drives the covariance to its Riccati steady state, the equal-caution sweep removes any global scale, and the tracker's margin stays within 5% of 1.10 times the class average on 99.26% of binding rows. RR(N) near zero follows from these three facts, and the paper itself offers the ratio check as a ground-truth-free substitute for the frontier analysis. What the decision-level machinery adds is the room and where it lies, and on the paper's own diagnostics neither is mainly about uncertainty. The oracle's margin is the realised error itself, so the room is nearly the class average's whole wrong-go area (5.6 of 6.0 points). Scrambling the oracle's per-decision scale with a log-normal factor of σ = 2 still leaves 0.82 of it. The room therefore lies in the within-decision pattern of the realised error, not in the per-decision scale that a covariance mainly controls. Only 38% of the pooled 3 s avoidable wrong-go remains when each phase is scored on its own frontier. Two-thirds of the braking prior's gain belongs to the cross-phase remainder, which no analysis isolates and whose size grows with how long the recordings ran after the stop. Under a reaction-and-stop commitment at the regulation's 0.75 s and 6 m/s², 3.7 decision-equivalents remain within phases. A simpler reading follows. A blind 3 s commitment punishes the constant-velocity predictor's lag behind a braking lead (mean error −3.2 m at 1 s). The constant-deceleration prior is the only arm that corrects the predicted mean, so it wins, and no variance arm, however informative, was given the same lever. On that reading, what lasts from the paper is the margin-ratio check and the warning that open-loop scores must state their commitment. Both are valuable, but they are narrower than three contributions and a located decision value.

### Ignored Alternative Explanations/Paths
1. Mean correction against variance. The gap between the braking prior and every covariance arm may reflect what each arm changes, not what each one knows: CK3 shifts the predicted mean, and all the other arms only scale a variance around a constant-velocity mean that lags a braking lead. No mean-shift arm uses the tracker's own information, and no variance arm encodes the braking assumption, so the comparison cannot separate information from mechanism.
2. A constant-acceleration mean prediction from the tracker's own acceleration estimate. Section VI says "the acceleration margin, which uses the tracker's acceleration estimate, recovers 38%, less than the data-free prior", but AM puts that estimate into a variance and CK3 puts its prior into the mean. The information comparison is confounded by mechanism.
3. A phase-aware class average, with one margin scale per phase, would test the paper's own reading that most of the pooled room comes from how one scale spends caution across phases.
4. A positive control in the covariance's own form: the per-decision position–velocity covariance, or a per-decision multiple of the class average, fitted to the realised error and projected exactly as N.
5. A two-sided adaptive covariance, an IMM tracker, or the planner-matched calibration of the author's own [9]. The tested NIS-inflated arm can only inflate a filter that is already too wide (median per-run mean NIS 0.24), and Section VI concedes that "An inflate-only rule cannot narrow it". It is therefore close to inert by construction (λ above 1 on 304 of 9,398 actor-decisions).

### Missing Stakeholder Perspectives
- Vehicles following the ego, which are exposed to the braking that larger margins or a braking prior induce (bears on M3).
- Non-braking traffic exposure, such as steady following, cut-ins and benign driving, which makes up most real driving and is absent from the evaluation (bears on M2).
- Occupants, whose comfort cost from fallback full braking is reported only for the closed-loop check, not for the open-loop arms.
- Safety-case and type-approval assessors, who need a margin scale fixed in advance rather than the best scale at each caution level that a frontier envelope picks in hindsight.

### Unexamined Premise
The equal-caution sweep gives every arm, at each caution level, the margin scale that does best on the evaluation outcomes. That is the right lens for asking whether per-decision variation carries information, and the Scope paragraph states the premise ("once the margin's scale is free"). It is not the lens of a deployed chance-constrained planner, which must fix its scale in advance, and there a calibrated absolute scale is the value a covariance offers. Section VI drops the qualifier ("a class average does as well as the tracker's covariance"). Because Scope states the premise, I do not raise it as a table row; the fix is to carry the qualifier into Sections VI and VII.

### Minor Points
- The NIS-inflated "distinguishable from zero" rests on percentile intervals for a non-smooth statistic, and the paper's own RR(N) point lies above its 97.5th percentile. The effect is small (anchor, text: Section IV-B "the NIS-inflated covariance's 0.09 points are about 7.7 of 8,290 decisions") and sits after the stop, not at the onset. A run-level sign-flip or permutation test should accompany the rule-based wording. Confidence 3, from the paper's bootstrap diagnostics.
- The word "room" means the 3 s oracle's advantage in the Notation table but the class average's avoidable wrong-go against a perfect oracle in the abstract's last clause (anchor, text: Abstract "too little room remains, phase by phase, for the braking prior's share to be measured"). The values nearly coincide as scored at 6 m/s², but the term should be used in one sense only. Confidence 4, from the definitions in Section IV-C.
- Section III-B defines a margin as a variance, but the 1.10 median of Fig. 2(b) matches the deviation ratio of Section V-D at long look-ahead (1.09 to 1.10), which suggests a distance ratio (anchor, text: Fig. 2 caption "N's margin over F0's on 152,264 binding rows"). State the units in the abstract and in Fig. 2. Confidence 3, from comparing Fig. 2(b) with Table S14.
- Pre-specification cannot be checked at review: hashes are cited, but the plan was not registered and the repository is a placeholder (anchor, text: Section IV-D "The plan was not registered with a third party"). Deposit the plan and lock manifests with the submission. Confidence 4.
- The amendments made after the results (anchor, table: Table S4, row "The abstract's last sentence") each narrow the plan's negative consequence sentence for the braking prior. Each edit is accurate, but all of them point the same way, and the paper should say so. Confidence 3.

### Observations (Non-Defects)
- The ratio of the tracker's margin to a class-average margin on binding rows is a genuinely portable diagnostic that needs no ground truth. It may be the paper's most reusable result.
- The graded oracles are an unusually honest check on the instrument. They are what reveal that the measure does not grade information within the lead-braking phase.
- The manuscript contains no instruction-injection text addressed to reviewers. Its bracketed placeholders are addressed to the author.
- Abstract, contribution and results numbers that I recomputed agree: the 383, 71 and 56 decisions, the 2.6 pooled-to-phase ratio and the 6% phase-weighted share.
