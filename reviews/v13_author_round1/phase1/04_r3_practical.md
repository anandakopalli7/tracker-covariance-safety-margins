## The eight answers

**1. Labels and sizes.** Largely right. "Confirmed" is used only for the original lead-braking sequence (V-A, Table 2: room, detection, equivalence, braking prediction, per-phase schedule). Amendment results carry "(A)", "passed (amendment)", "failed", "replicated"; the Table 6 caption defines "(A)". Holm is reported beside the labels and changes none: six second-amendment primaries (H rows, 0.0003 to 0.0079) and, separately, the two third-amendment tests (0.99 and 0.18, both failed). Sizes are honest: shares are given as 2.8%, 3.0%, 2.2%, 4.6%, "small by that margin". One note: Table 6 uses "(A)" where the text says "passed (amendment)", which is fine once the caption is read. The equivalence margin is 5% of an oracle's room, and "adds nothing" in the abstract is stronger than "adds no more than 0.15% of the room" (the interval top).

**2. Overreach.**
- *Dropout gain's source:* handled. "A margin that only counts missed updates reproduces the dropout gain" is supported by the coast-count control (difference −0.0024, Holm 0.99), with the caveat that the factors were fitted from the covariance margin's own median ratios (V-E) and that ranking among dropout decisions is "not shown at 2 Hz". The 10 Hz case is not compared. Fine.
- *Closed loop:* the text states "matched in open-loop caution" only, the equal-time-lost comparison failed (−0.05 [−0.12, 0.02]), and the class average missed calibration. The abstract still leads with "21% against 33%" (R3-4).
- *nuScenes mechanism check:* correctly "weak", "slightly inversely over all pairs", population chosen on training split (stated), "partly a level effect". Fine.
- *Real-log decision test:* "no room, so nothing was tested". Fine.
- *Two-noise IMM:* "holds for the position-velocity interface only; a wider interface was not tested" (V-E, VI). Fine.
- *Scope of the null:* "constant-velocity prediction", "one in-house planner", "whether a covariance adds anything on top of the braking prediction's path was not tested" are stated. Remaining over-read risk is for the practitioner: the abstract's "does not justify removing a covariance margin" (R3-1) and the missing speed range (R3-3).

**3. Earlier limits.**
- (a) Closed loop matched in open-loop caution only: **partly addressed**. Equal-time-lost comparison added and failed; remains one level, one point on the class-average frontier, 135 cases, a class average at 33% contacts.
- (b) Dropout gain might be a coasting margin: **addressed**. The control was run, failed, and the claim was rewritten; remains that the factors derive from the covariance and the 10 Hz case was not compared.
- (c) Abstract and conclusion stronger than body: **mostly addressed**. "weakly", "slightly inversely", "were not shown" now appear. Remains "adds nothing", "reproduces the dropout gain", and the cost-free 78% (R3-1).
- (d) Two-noise IMM interface: **addressed by scope sentences** (V-E, VI); a wider interface remains untested.
- (e) Holm and recount scope: **addressed** (IV-E: which sequences Holm covers; recount scope "every value printed in the main text and all but the marked (‡) values of the supplement").
- (f) Density, round-2 results only in the supplement: **partly addressed**. Round 2 is now in V-E and Table 6; the price is a very dense V-E (R3-13).
- Structural limits, none fixable by wording: a confirmed result that could hardly fail (the null for a near-constant covariance is expected from [26]); small positive effects (2% to 5%); one simulator and one configuration; no decision-level real-log evidence (no room); the process (amendments after results, local hash locks, AI authorship). The text discloses all five honestly; none is removed.

**4. Readability.** The abstract (245 words, no abbreviations) opens with the setup and gives the bottom line in sentence 4; the practical message is at the end, in hedged form. About a dozen coined terms (wrong go, room, share, equal caution, amplitude-matched ranking oracle, recovers, uninformative variation, amplitude-limited, coast-count, level effect) are defined in Table 1 or in IV, except "level effect" and "steady decisions" (R3-10). Every claim has main-text evidence: abstract figures map to Tables 2, 3, 4, 6. A practitioner has to read three pages of definitions before the first result.
