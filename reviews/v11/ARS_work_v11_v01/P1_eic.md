## Contract Paraphrase

D1 (methodology_rigor, mandatory). From the editorial chair, the first mandatory test asks whether the empirical work under the manuscript would survive a methods specialist's scrutiny: the design has to suit the question being asked, the handling of data has to be traceable, the statistics have to be reported fully enough to be checked, and another group should be able to repeat the work from what is supplied. The methodology seat owns and scores this; I do not score it, but a failure here would limit any recommendation the panel can make.

D2 (domain_accuracy, mandatory). The second mandatory test is whether the manuscript is right about its own field: what it asserts should agree with the present state of the evidence, the earlier work it cites should be described faithfully, and specialist vocabulary and reported findings should be used without error. The domain seat owns and scores this; I note it so that my judgement of fit and contribution does not double-count domain mistakes.

D3 (argumentative_coherence, mandatory). The third mandatory test concerns the logic of the case being made: the central thesis must hold together, the evidence offered must genuinely carry the claims resting on it, no reasoning flaw may undercut the main line, and every premise a core claim depends on, including any premise about who gains, who pays, or who is excluded, must be supported rather than assumed. The devil's advocate seat owns this, with the methodology seat also eligible; I do not score it.

D4 (cross_disciplinary_relevance, high). This high-priority test asks whether a reader trained in a neighbouring discipline could follow how the problem is framed, what the key terms mean, and what follows from the results, and whether any claim that reaches across disciplinary lines is backed rather than gestured at. The perspective seat owns and scores it; I do not.

D5 (writing_and_structure, normal). This normal-priority dimension is one I score: it covers how the manuscript is organised, how clearly it explains itself, whether its figures and tables are legible and earn their space, and whether it follows the presentation conventions a journal of this kind expects of authors. From an editorial standpoint, problems here are usually repairable and seldom decide the outcome alone, unless they stop reviewers from checking the work.

D6 (venue_fit_and_contribution, mandatory). This mandatory dimension is also mine: it asks whether the manuscript belongs in the configured venue and whether it adds something new and important enough for that venue's readers. Because no criteria manifest or target brief accompanies this call, I reserve this judgement entirely for the paper-visible phase and make no claim about fit here; the plan below only fixes in advance what evidence would move the score in each direction.

## Scoring Plan

### D5: writing_and_structure
dimension_id: D5
what_to_look_for: Whether the section order lets a reader move from problem to approach to evidence to conclusion without backtracking; whether every figure and table is cited, labelled, legible and necessary; whether terms, symbols and abbreviations are defined once and used consistently; whether length is proportionate to the contribution; and whether references, figures and sections follow the venue's author conventions.
what_triggers_block: Organisation or exposition is so unclear or internally inconsistent that a reviewer cannot locate or verify the main claims, or key figures or tables are missing, unreadable, or contradict the text, so the manuscript needs substantial restructuring before it can be fairly assessed.
what_triggers_warn: The manuscript is reviewable but carries correctable presentation defects, such as redundant or overlong sections, inconsistent notation or terminology, figures or tables that are hard to read or thinly captioned, or departures from venue formatting conventions.

### D6: venue_fit_and_contribution
dimension_id: D6
what_to_look_for: Whether the stated problem and intended audience fall inside the configured venue's scope; whether the manuscript names a specific contribution and positions it against the closest prior work so that novelty can be checked; whether its significance for the venue's readers is argued with evidence rather than asserted; and whether the contribution claims stay within what the reported evidence supports.
what_triggers_block: The topic is within scope, but the contribution as presented is too incremental, poorly positioned against prior work, or overstated relative to its evidence for the venue's readership, and could plausibly reach the bar through repositioning, narrowed claims, or added evidence in a major revision.
what_triggers_warn: Fit and an original contribution are both present, but the novelty statement, the comparison with the closest prior work, or the case for significance to the venue's readers is weaker or less explicit than it should be and needs sharpening.
what_triggers_fatal: The manuscript lies outside the configured venue's scope, or it offers no identifiable original contribution beyond existing published work, so that no revision of this submission could make it suitable for this venue.
