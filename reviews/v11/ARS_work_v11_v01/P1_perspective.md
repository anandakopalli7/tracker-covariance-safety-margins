## Contract Paraphrase

D1 (methodology_rigor): This dimension asks whether the empirical design, the way data are generated and processed, the statistical reporting, and the material offered for replication would satisfy referees in the field. Seen from an assurance-practice vantage it matters because a result that cannot be rerun or audited cannot be carried into a safety case or a regulatory evidence file. It is a mandatory gate owned by the methodology seat, and I plan no score for it.

D2 (domain_accuracy): This dimension asks whether the claims agree with the current body of evidence in the field, whether cited earlier work is described faithfully, and whether technical vocabulary and reported results are free of factual error. Readers arriving from neighbouring disciplines are especially exposed to misused terminology because they cannot correct it themselves. It is mandatory and belongs to the domain seat; I do not score it.

D3 (argumentative_coherence): This dimension asks whether the central thesis hangs together: the evidence offered really carries the claims, no reasoning error undercuts the main argument, and no load-bearing premise is left unsupported, including premises about which parties gain, which absorb the cost, and which are left out. That last clause touches my practical lens, since any safety-relevant decision rule redistributes risk among road users, but the dimension is mandatory and is scored by the devil's-advocate and methodology seats, not by me.

D4 (cross_disciplinary_relevance): This is the dimension I own. It asks whether a reader from a neighbouring field, such as safety assurance and type approval, decision analysis, or estimation and control, can follow the framing, the definitions, and the stated implications without insider knowledge, and whether any claim that reaches across disciplinary lines is backed by argument or evidence rather than simply asserted. In practice I will check whether the key quantities are defined well enough for a safety engineer to map them onto an established assurance vocabulary, whether any claimed practical, standards, or regulatory relevance is earned, and whether decision-analytic language is used in its accepted sense. It is high priority but not mandatory, so it carries no fatal class.

D5 (writing_and_structure): This dimension asks whether the manuscript is well organised and clearly written, whether its figures and tables communicate, and whether it follows the target outlet's conventions. Clear exposition also limits how far an adjacent-field reader can get, but this normal-priority dimension is scored by the editor-in-chief seat; I plan no score.

D6 (venue_fit_and_contribution): This dimension asks whether the work suits the intended venue and offers an original contribution whose significance matches that venue's readership. Practical significance to industry or regulators is one input to that judgement, but the call is a mandatory gate owned by the editor-in-chief seat, and I do not score it.

## Scoring Plan

### D4: cross_disciplinary_relevance
dimension_id: D4
what_to_look_for: Whether core terms, metrics, and the decision framing are defined so that safety-assurance, regulatory, and decision-analysis readers can follow them, and whether each claim of practical, standards, or cross-field relevance is backed by an explicit mapping, argument, or evidence rather than assertion.
what_triggers_block: A central claim of practical, regulatory, or cross-field significance rests on a mapping or premise that is absent or contradicted, or a core metric or decision concept is undefined or used against its accepted meaning in the adjacent field so that an outside reader would reach a materially wrong conclusion.
what_triggers_warn: Adjacent-field connections are asserted with only partial support, some key terms or implications need insider knowledge to interpret, or the limits on transferring the findings to deployment or assurance practice are left implicit.
