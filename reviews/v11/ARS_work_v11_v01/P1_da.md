## Contract Paraphrase

D1 (methodology_rigor): Read adversarially, this dimension asks whether the study's design, its handling of data, its statistical reporting and the means it gives others to reproduce the work would withstand a hostile referee in this field. The challenge is not whether a method exists but whether a skeptic could rerun it, whether the reported numbers carry their uncertainty, and whether a design choice quietly makes the favoured result inevitable. It is mandatory and owned by the methodology seat; I do not score it, but any methodological weakness that a core argument leans on becomes relevant to my own coherence challenge.

D2 (domain_accuracy): From the adversarial side, this dimension tests whether every claim matches what the domain currently knows, whether earlier work is described the way its authors would recognise it, and whether technical terms and reported results are free of factual error. A devil's advocate presses on any place where a strawmanned predecessor, an outdated finding or a misused term is doing argumentative work. It is mandatory and owned by the domain seat; I do not score it.

D3 (argumentative_coherence): This is my owned, mandatory dimension. It asks whether the central thesis holds together without internal contradiction, whether the evidence presented actually reaches the conclusions drawn from it, whether any fallacy undermines the main line of argument, and whether every premise a core claim depends on is supported, explicitly including premises about who gains, who pays or carries the risk, and who is excluded. My task is to attack the argument at its weakest load-bearing joint, construct the strongest counter-explanation, and judge whether the headline conclusions still follow once that pressure is applied.

D4 (cross_disciplinary_relevance): Adversarially, this dimension asks whether a reader from a neighbouring field could follow the framing, definitions and implications without insider knowledge, and whether any claim that reaches across disciplines is backed rather than asserted. The skeptic's question is whether interdisciplinary reach is earned or merely announced. It is high priority and owned by the perspective seat; I do not score it.

D5 (writing_and_structure): This dimension concerns how the manuscript is organised, how clearly it explains itself, the quality of its figures and tables, and whether it follows the venue's conventions. From a challenge stance, the relevant risk is presentation that obscures a weak step in the reasoning or lets a figure imply more than the text supports. It is normal priority and owned by the editor-in-chief seat; I do not score it.

D6 (venue_fit_and_contribution): Adversarially, this dimension asks whether the work belongs in the configured venue and whether its contribution is genuinely new and large enough to matter to that readership, rather than an incremental or repackaged result. A devil's advocate asks what would be lost if the paper were never published. It is mandatory and owned by the editor-in-chief seat; I do not score it and make no venue-alignment claim here.

## Scoring Plan

### D3: argumentative_coherence
dimension_id: D3
what_to_look_for: Trace each headline claim back to the evidence offered for it and test for circular reasoning, internal contradiction, generalisation beyond the conditions actually examined, selective or asymmetric comparisons, causal language the design cannot support, unstated load-bearing premises (including premises about who benefits, who bears cost or risk, and who is left out), and whether the strongest rival explanation is named and answered.
what_triggers_block: A core claim rests on a fallacy, an internal contradiction, an unsupported load-bearing premise (including one about who benefits, who bears cost, or who is left out), or a generalisation beyond what the evidence covers, so the central argument does not hold as stated, yet it could be repaired by narrowing the claims, adding the missing support, or answering the strongest counter-argument.
what_triggers_warn: Localised coherence gaps that leave the central argument standing: a secondary claim stretched modestly past its evidence, overstated wording that the evidence only partly supports, a plausible alternative explanation acknowledged but only partly addressed, or a premise about beneficiaries, cost bearers or excluded parties that is thinly supported for a non-core claim.
what_triggers_fatal: The central thesis is self-refuting, contradicted by the evidence the manuscript itself reports, or dependent on a circular or demonstrably false premise, so that no revision short of replacing the core argument could make the headline conclusion follow.
