> Do Kalman-tracker covariances improve planning decisions as safety margins? The room is how far an oracle margin cuts wrong-go decisions below a class-average margin at equal caution. On fresh CARLA runs whose lead brakes to a stop, scored once under a locked plan, the room is 5.58 [4.99, 6.17] percentage points, and the score detects rank information in a ±5% band. The fixed-noise tracker's covariance varies less: at its own spread an oracle recovers 0.01%, and its share, within [−0.09%, 0.15%], is equivalent to a class-average margin within 5% of the room. An interacting-multiple-model tracker as tuned here barely varies (99.7% of rows within 5% of its median); an adaptive-noise filter recovers 4.6% of its room (descriptive). A braking prediction recovers 78% of the pooled room and a per-phase schedule 92%, costing progress behind leads that do not brake hard, though both lower wrong goes behind stop-and-go leads. In closed loop behind braking leads (lower level), the fixed-noise covariance is equivalent (±0.10 contact share) and the prediction is safer at no extra cost (0.16 s lost), as is the time gap (descriptive). Cut-in events and held-out nuScenes logs showed no measurable room to test. Magnitudes are at 2 Hz without latency; at 10 Hz and 0.1 s the after-stop room falls to 45%.
